CRM Integration

Send Website Leads Into Your CRM With the Context Sales Needs

A lead should not lose useful information the moment someone submits a form. Shivantra connects website enquiries, bookings and lead journeys with CRM systems so contacts can be created or updated with the information your sales process actually needs.

Email is a notification, not a lead-management system

A website form that sends an email may be enough for a very small process. As lead volume grows, email-only handling can create:

  • duplicate entry
  • missing source data
  • delayed follow-up
  • unclear ownership
  • inconsistent records
  • limited reporting

CRM integration moves the lead into the system where sales already works.

The integration can do more than create a contact

  2. Find an existing contact
  3. Create or update the record
  4. Create a lead/deal/opportunity
  5. Preserve campaign information
  6. Assign the lead
  7. Notify sales
  8. Send the visitor a confirmation
  9. Trigger follow-up tasks

Deduplication

New form submission does not always mean new person

A visitor may submit:

  • several forms
  • a new enquiry months later
  • a different campaign form
  • the same email with updated information

The integration should define how records are matched. Possible keys include:

  • email
  • phone
  • CRM identifier
  • company/domain
  • combination rules

Deduplication logic should match the CRM model rather than blindly creating a new contact every time.

Lead routing

Route leads based on the information already collected

Assignment can depend on:

  • location
  • service
  • product
  • account ownership
  • language
  • industry
  • franchise
  • territory

Routing can be handled:

  • by the website integration
  • by CRM workflow
  • by an automation platform

We choose the layer that keeps the logic easiest to maintain.

Campaign attribution

Keep campaign information attached to the lead

If a visitor arrives from a campaign and later submits a form, source information can be preserved and sent to the CRM where appropriate. This can include:

  • UTM values
  • original landing page
  • referrer
  • advertising platform identifiers
  • first/last-touch fields depending on the chosen attribution model

The goal is to avoid a CRM full of leads whose source is simply “Website.”

Closing the loop

The CRM can also send useful outcomes back to advertising and analytics systems

For businesses where the meaningful conversion happens after the website form, lead quality may be more important than form-submission volume. Examples:

  • qualified
  • meeting completed
  • opportunity created
  • deal won

Where platform capabilities and privacy requirements allow, offline outcomes can be sent back to advertising or analytics systems to improve measurement.

Google's current enhanced-conversions model supports first-party lead data and offline lead outcomes as part of conversion measurement, subject to its customer-data policies and implementation requirements.

Do not collect or send data without the required legal basis or consent.

Security & privacy

Lead data needs careful handling

Implementation should consider:

  • HTTPS
  • server-side credentials
  • required consent
  • data minimization
  • retention
  • CRM permissions
  • file-upload security
  • masking sensitive logs
  • platform-specific customer-data policies

Marketing consent and sales-enquiry processing may have different legal bases depending on jurisdiction and implementation.

Do not combine them automatically without appropriate review.

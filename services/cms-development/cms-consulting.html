CMS Consulting

Choose the CMS After You Understand the Requirement

WordPress, headless CMS, custom CMS, SaaS platform, open source. The number of options makes it easy to start by comparing software before defining what the organization actually needs. Shivantra provides CMS consulting around content, editorial workflow, architecture, integrations and migration so the technology decision follows the requirement rather than leading it.

CMS selection often starts in the wrong place

A feature comparison cannot tell you how your team needs to work

Platforms publish long feature lists.

Those lists can be useful, but they do not answer questions such as:

  • Who edits content?
  • How many people publish?
  • What needs approval?
  • Is content reused?
  • How many languages exist?
  • Does the frontend need to be independent?
  • Which systems provide data?
  • What does the internal technical team want to own?
  • How difficult will migration be?
  • What will the platform cost to operate over several years?

A CMS can be powerful and still be wrong for your organization.

Migration

How much existing content, metadata, media and URL history needs to move?

Cost

Traditional CMS

Traditional CMS can be the practical choice

A traditional CMS can work well when:

  • the website is the primary channel
  • editors need familiar page-oriented workflows
  • the project benefits from an established ecosystem
  • architecture should remain relatively simple

The platform still needs careful implementation. Plugin-heavy or unrestricted page-builder setups can create their own maintenance problems.

Headless CMS

Headless CMS is useful when content and frontend need independence

Headless architecture can make sense when:

  • structured content is important
  • several frontends consume content
  • a custom React/Next.js experience is required
  • APIs and integrations are central
  • teams need to reuse content across channels

It also creates more responsibility for frontend development, preview and delivery architecture.

Custom CMS

Custom CMS is justified by custom operations, not by preference

A custom CMS may be appropriate when:

  • workflow is proprietary
  • permissions are unusually granular
  • content is deeply linked to operational data
  • the CMS is part of an internal software platform

It also creates long-term ownership responsibilities.

Hybrid architecture

Sometimes the right answer is a combination

A business may use:

  • CMS for marketing content
  • ERP for product data
  • CRM for customer records
  • DAM for media
  • ecommerce platform for transactions

Trying to move all information into the CMS can create duplication. A better architecture may define which system owns each type of data and connect them through APIs.

CMS consulting should therefore consider the whole information environment rather than evaluating the CMS in isolation.

Total cost of ownership

Platform license is only one line in the cost

A lower license price can still produce a more expensive system if it requires:

  • extensive custom development
  • frequent manual maintenance
  • specialist hosting
  • many paid extensions
  • difficult upgrades
  • inefficient editorial workflows

Likewise, a higher platform subscription can sometimes reduce infrastructure or maintenance effort. We consider:

  • licensing
  • implementation
  • hosting
  • maintenance
  • upgrades
  • developer requirements
  • migration
  • content-team effort

“Which CMS costs less today?”

is the wrong question. The relevant one is: “Which approach makes sense to operate for the expected life of the platform?”

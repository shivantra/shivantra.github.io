Website Redesign

Redesign the Website Without Throwing Away What Already Works

A website can become outdated long before the business behind it does. Shivantra redesigns websites across structure, content experience, design, CMS, performance and technology while accounting for the pages, URLs and systems your existing website may already depend on.

A redesign should solve a reason

A new look is not enough

Businesses usually start thinking about a redesign because something has changed. The company may have grown, the services may be different or the brand may have evolved. The website may be difficult to update, slow on mobile or increasingly dependent on old technology, and customers may struggle to understand what the company actually offers.

A redesign should identify those problems first.

Otherwise, it is easy to produce a newer-looking website that carries the same structural problems into a new interface.

Common reasons to redesign

When a redesign makes sense

The website no longer represents the business

Services, positioning, team or brand have changed while the website stayed the same.

Visitors struggle to find information

Navigation, page hierarchy or content has accumulated without a clear structure.

The mobile experience is weak

The site technically shrinks to a phone but is difficult to read, navigate or use.

Your team cannot manage content efficiently

Routine updates require developers, workarounds or risky page-builder editing.

Performance has deteriorated

Large media, scripts, plugins and legacy implementation can create increasingly heavy pages.

The technology has become a constraint

The platform or codebase may make ordinary improvements unnecessarily difficult.

Lead paths are unclear

Forms, calls to action or service journeys may not reflect how customers actually buy.

You need to migrate or replatform

A new CMS or technical architecture may make sense because of changing editorial or technical requirements.

Two projects

A responsible redesign is really two projects

The visible project is the new website. The less visible project is protecting and migrating the value the existing website has already accumulated. That may include:

  • useful content
  • indexed URLs
  • backlinks
  • search visibility
  • analytics history
  • metadata
  • documents
  • forms
  • integrations
  • tracking
  • established user journeys

Changing all of those carelessly can create problems even when the new visual design is excellent.

Before we redesign

We first understand what already exists

An existing website provides evidence. Before changing it, we may review:

Content

Which pages are current, duplicated, outdated or genuinely useful?

Search visibility

Which pages receive organic traffic or have external links?

URLs

Which addresses should stay, which should change and what needs redirecting?

Analytics

Which journeys and pages are actually used?

Conversions

Where do enquiries or important actions originate?

Technology

What is making the site difficult to change or maintain?

CMS

Can editors manage content safely and efficiently?

Integrations

Which forms, analytics, APIs or other systems must continue working?

This creates a stronger basis for deciding what to keep, improve, consolidate or remove.

Migration

URL changes need a plan

If a redesign changes page URLs, search engines and users need a clear route from the old location to the new one. Where appropriate, we prepare:

  • old-to-new URL mapping
  • server-side permanent redirects
  • updated internal links
  • canonical URLs
  • updated sitemaps
  • metadata
  • structured-data review
  • Search Console checks
  • post-launch crawl review

Not every URL needs to change. Keeping useful URLs stable can often simplify the migration.

If pages are consolidated, redirects should lead to the most relevant replacement rather than sending everything to the homepage.

Search expectations

A redesign can be planned carefully, but search performance should not be "guaranteed"

Search engines need time to recrawl and process significant site changes. Even a well-managed migration can experience temporary movement while new URLs and signals are processed. Our role is to reduce avoidable risk through:

  • correct redirects
  • clear URL mapping
  • preserved or improved content
  • updated internal links
  • valid canonicals
  • sitemaps
  • crawl checks
  • post-launch monitoring

We do not sell redesigns with promises that no ranking will ever move.

Refresh, rebuild or replatform?

Not every website needs the same level of change

Refresh

Best when the foundation is sound

Best when the structure and technology are fundamentally sound but the interface or content needs improvement.

Rebuild

Best when the structure needs to change

Best when the existing implementation has become difficult to maintain or the new structure requires substantial changes.

Replatform

Best when the platform is the limitation

Best when the current CMS or architecture is the main limitation.

The correct choice depends on:

  • current platform
  • editing needs
  • technical debt
  • content
  • integrations
  • future requirements
  • migration risk

A redesign engagement should begin by determining which level of change is justified.

Corporate Website Development

Corporate Websites Built for Scale, Structure and Change

As organizations grow, their websites usually become more complex too. More services. More locations. More teams. More content. More stakeholders. More systems. Shivantra designs and develops corporate websites with the structure, content model and technical foundation needed to keep that complexity manageable.

Discuss Your Corporate Website
A corporate website is a system, not just a collection of pages

A corporate site may be used by customers, prospects, partners, employees, candidates, investors, media and suppliers. Each group may need different information.

At the same time, internal teams may need different levels of control over what gets created, reviewed and published. If the website is designed one page at a time, complexity accumulates quickly.

That is why we start with the system underneath the pages:

  • information architecture
  • content model
  • design system
  • CMS roles
  • publishing workflow
  • integrations
  • localization
  • technical architecture

This creates a website that can continue growing without becoming harder to manage with every new section.

What corporate websites need to handle

Designed for organizational complexity

Multiple services and business units

Large organizations often need to explain several offerings without fragmenting the user experience. We organize services and business units into a clear hierarchy while preserving relevant cross-links.

Multiple locations

Location information can be structured for countries, regions, offices, branches, plants or service areas.

Multiple audiences

Customers, partners, candidates and other stakeholders should not all be forced through the same journey.

Multiple content teams

Content roles and permissions can be designed around who creates, reviews and publishes information.

Multiple languages

Localization should be planned in the content model and workflow rather than added as an afterthought.

Multiple systems

Corporate websites may need to work with CRM, ERP, recruitment, analytics, identity, digital asset or other business systems.

Governance

Give teams the right amount of control

The challenge is not simply making content editable. It is making the right content editable by the right people in the right way. A corporate CMS may need:

  • Contributor roles
  • Editor roles
  • Publisher roles
  • Approval workflows
  • Preview
  • Scheduled publishing
  • Reusable content
  • Locked brand components
  • Localized content
  • Audit history where supported

The exact model depends on the platform and organization.

The goal is to support content teams without allowing the website to lose consistency over time.

Multilingual and multi-market websites

Localization is more than translating page text

International websites may require differences in:

  • Language
  • Terminology
  • Services
  • Regulatory information
  • Contact details
  • Campaigns
  • URLs
  • Local teams
  • Media
  • Calls to action

A scalable setup should allow common content to remain consistent while giving local teams enough flexibility for genuine market differences.

We plan localization requirements alongside the content model, CMS and URL strategy rather than treating translation as a final production task.

Consistent design at scale

A design system helps the website grow without becoming inconsistent

Corporate websites often involve many page authors over several years. Without reusable patterns, each new page can slowly introduce:

  • New spacing
  • New button styles
  • New layouts
  • Inconsistent typography
  • Duplicated components

We develop reusable page sections and visual rules so new content can be created within a controlled system.

The aim is not to make every page look identical. It is to make the experience feel like one organization.

Integrations

Keep the corporate website connected to the organization behind it

The website does not need to duplicate data already managed in another system. Where appropriate, integrations can allow the site to receive or send data to:

  • CRM
  • ERP
  • Recruitment systems
  • Marketing automation
  • Analytics
  • Identity systems
  • Document platforms
  • Internal APIs

This reduces manual duplication and helps the website become part of the wider digital environment.

Migration

Replacing an existing corporate website requires a migration plan

A redesign or replatform should account for what the current website has already accumulated:

  • Indexed URLs
  • Search traffic
  • Backlinks
  • Content
  • Documents
  • Metadata
  • Analytics
  • Integrations

Where URLs change, the project should include a clear mapping between old and new locations and appropriate redirects.

Content should be reviewed before migration rather than copied blindly.

The objective is to improve the website without discarding useful information or creating unnecessary disruption.

Process

Our approach to corporate website development

01

Discovery and stakeholder alignment

Understand business units, audiences, governance, markets and technical constraints.

02

Content and platform audit

Review existing content, analytics, CMS, integrations and technical issues where applicable.

03

Information architecture

04

Content model and CMS design

Determine reusable content types, roles, workflows and localization.

05

UX and design system

Design key journeys and reusable components.

06

Development and integrations

Implement the website, CMS and required system connections.

07

Migration and QA

Move approved content, implement redirects, test functionality and review important URLs.

08

Launch and governance handover

Deploy, monitor and give internal teams the process and permissions needed to operate the site.

Quality requirements

Performance and accessibility are part of the foundation

Large sites can accumulate heavy scripts, media and third-party tools over time. We consider:

  • Responsive performance
  • Asset optimization
  • Semantic markup
  • Keyboard interaction
  • Focus states
  • Forms
  • Content readability
  • Maintainable components
  • Third-party script impact

Accessibility requirements should be defined explicitly for organizations that need a particular conformance level.

We do not make untested compliance claims.
